# 3. Position: World-Local Logical Spaces

To address the limitations of continuous representability, we propose an architecture centered on locally scoped logical spaces. We explicitly reject the historical ambition of building universal, flat ontologies that attempt to map the entirety of human knowledge. Such systems inevitably fail under the weight of polysemy and the fluidity of context. Instead, we advocate for knowledge representation within restricted "local worlds," which act as discrete coordinate systems defined by domain-specific dimensions of discrimination. This approach allows us to decouple the linguistic sign from its logical symbol. While an LLM may process the word "carrot" as a common sign across various contexts, the underlying representational framework projects that sign onto distinct coordinates depending on whether the active world is "botany" or "cooking." 

In this model, meaning is not an absolute property of a vector but a valid projection authorized by a local grammar of sense ($\Gamma$). Each local world defines its own logical coordinates, and propositions are only admissible if they can be projected into that space. If a candidate proposition—generated by an LLM—cannot be mapped onto the dimensions of the local world, it is rejected as *unsinnig* (nonsense) before it can even be evaluated for truth. By modeling states of affairs as typed, atomic propositions rather than flat text, we establish a symbolic validation layer that enforces representational rigor and ensures that only senseful assertions are entered into the knowledge base.
