# State of the Art: Wittgenstein and Artificial Intelligence

This collection gathers published academic research focused on the intersection of the *Tractatus Logico-Philosophicus*, neural networks, knowledge representation, and AI.

## 1. Utilizing OWL for Wittgenstein's Tractatus
- **URL:** [https://wab.uib.no/ojs/index.php/agora-alws/article/view/2675](https://wab.uib.no/ojs/index.php/agora-alws/article/view/2675)
- **Author:** Zöllner-Weber
- **Abstract:** This article presents the experience gained in attempting to develop a computational ontology for part of Wittgenstein's *Tractatus* using Semantic Web technologies. Using the OWL standard, the ontology structures information hierarchically to support semantic retrieval and automated reasoning.
- **Why it is relevant:** It represents the classic computational science approach to the *Tractatus*, proving that its logical-hierarchical structure is inherently programmable.
- **Why it is distinct from our approach:** This and similar projects (such as the *Wittgenstein Ontology Project*) treat the *Tractatus* as the *subject* to be modeled within a flat, universal database (OWL). Our system, instead, uses the Tractarian ontology as the *underlying architectural engine* to build its own database of dynamic local logical spaces. We do not "program the book"; we program *with* the rules of the book.

## 2. Wittgenstein's Tractatus, the Vienna Circle, and Artificial Neural Networks as the First Computational Theory of Mind (2023)
- **URL:** [https://www.researchgate.net/publication/374910532_Wittgenstein's_Tractatus_the_Vienna_Circle_and_Artificial_Neural_Networks_as_the_First_Computational_Theory_of_Mind_Does_Development_of_AI_Owe_Anything_to_Wittgenstein](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/374910532_Wittgenstein's_Tractatus_the_Vienna_Circle_and_Artificial_Neural_Networks_as_the_First_Computational_Theory_of_Mind_Does_Development_of_AI_Owe_Anything_to_Wittgenstein)
- **Abstract:** By examining the influence of Wittgenstein's *Tractatus* on Carnap's "Logical Syntax" and the Vienna Circle, it is possible to see this work as a distant but fundamental starting point in the development of artificial intelligence. The article traces the historical line of how this logical atomism inspired McCulloch and Pitts in the creation of the first artificial neural networks.
- **Why it is relevant:** It provides undeniable academic backing for our position: the *Tractatus* is not just philosophy; it is the first "computational theory of mind" and the mathematical foundation of AI.
- **Why it is distinct from our approach:** This is an article of theoretical-historical analysis that explains how logic derived into neural networks. Our work is an active engineering proposal: it seeks to *reverse* the historical abandonment of the strict deterministic route in favor of the current stochastic route. We use Contextualized Boolean Algebra to force neural networks (LLMs) to submit again to strict propositional logic in neuro-symbolic environments.

## 3. Wittgenstein in the Machine (Critical Inquiry, Vol. 47, No. 3 - Lydia H. Liu, 2021)
- **URL:** [https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/10.1086/713551](https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/10.1086/713551)
- **Abstract:** This article brings to light how AI research has benefited from Wittgensteinian philosophy since the beginnings of computing in 1950. It focuses on the Cambridge Language Research Unit (CLRU) and its founder, Margaret Masterman, whose pioneering contributions in machine translation, information retrieval, and knowledge representation technologies were deeply inspired by Wittgenstein's philosophy of language.
- **Why it is relevant:** It demonstrates that a validated historical and methodological precedent exists in computer science for applying Wittgenstein to Information Retrieval and Natural Language Processing (NLP).
- **Why it is distinct from our approach:** Masterman's pioneering team in the 1950s focused primarily on the *late Wittgenstein* (language games and contextual use) to generate heuristics for text translation. We operate strictly on the *early Wittgenstein* (the logical atomism of the *Tractatus*, States of Affairs encoded in bits, and the limits of sense), achieving an executable mathematical formalization oriented toward disambiguation and hallucination elimination.

## 4. Language Models and the Private Language Argument: A Wittgensteinian Guide to Machine Learning (Cambridge University Press, 2025/2026)
- **URL:** [https://resolve.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/D8D89946EEF76DD542DB7ECFA8B2236F/9781839991387c7_p145-164.pdf](https://resolve.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/D8D89946EEF76DD542DB7ECFA8B2236F/9781839991387c7_p145-164.pdf)
- **Abstract:** Investigates NLP systems and the development of Deep Learning neural networks (including embeddings like Word2Vec) under a Wittgensteinian light. It explores how contemporary language models deduce representations and meanings from probabilistic context and contrasts this with the conception of the *Notebooks*, the *Tractatus*, and *Philosophical Investigations*.
- **Why it is relevant:** It is the absolute vanguard in contemporary debate: it explicitly compares continuous word embeddings with Wittgenstein's philosophy to understand the limits of foundation models.
- **Why it is distinct from our approach:** This work uses Wittgenstein's philosophy to perform a *theoretical critique* of how the stochastic embeddings of LLMs function. Our research takes the leap toward a *constructive algorithmic solution*: we do not only criticize continuous spaces, but mathematically build the functional model of algebraic "Masks" and boolean matrices indexed in $O(1)$, providing the code architecture and the LISP prototype that materializes the alternative.
