# Knowledge Representation: Classical Lineage and Modern Symbolic Bridges

The debate over semantic representation is rooted in decades of Knowledge Representation (KR) research. Our position builds on this lineage while proposing a radical shift toward locally scoped representability.

## 1. Classical Semantic Foundations

- **Brachman (AAAI 1990):** *The Future of Knowledge Representation.* A foundational text that outlines the transition from early semantic networks and frames to more formal production systems and predicate logic. 
    *   *Utility:* Provides the historical context to argue that our work is not a naive return to symbolic AI, but a refined application of KR principles to the limits of modern neural systems.
- **Lehmann (1992):** *Semantic Networks.* A comprehensive review of "meaning as graph structure," covering inheritance, frame systems, and logical extensions.
- **Sowa (1991):** *Principles of Semantic Networks.* Connects semantic representation to deep problems of meaning, metonymy, and the engineering of conceptual links.
    *   *Utility:* Establishes the complexity of defining "meaning," which supports our argument that continuous embeddings oversimplify semantics by conflating similarity with sense.

## 2. The Symbolic-Neural Divide

- **Ferrone & Zanzotto (Frontiers in Robotics and AI, 2019):** *Symbolic, Distributed, and Distributional Representations for NLP in the Era of Deep Learning.* This survey is critical for our framing; it explicitly compares the three representational paradigms and calls for a renewal of the link between symbols and deep networks.
- **Computational Semantics Survey (Wiley 2011):** Discusses the capture of meaning in representations suitable for automated inference. It aligns with our goal of creating a "representational layer" that supports formal validation.

## 3. Ontologies and Knowledge Graphs

- **Schneider (2020):** *Ontologies and Data Management.* Surveys Description Logics (DL) and data heterogeneity. 
    *   *Utility:* Used to contrast our "Local World" approach against the failures of universal, globalized ontologies in handling context and polysemy.
- **Simperl et al. (AACL 2024):** *Knowledge Graphs in Natural Language Processing.* Reviews the integration of KGs with modern NLP.
    *   *Utility:* Serves as a baseline for "Alternative Views." While KGs provide grounding, they typically do not enforce a local grammar of sense *before* an assertion is made.

## 4. Synthesizing the Lineage

Knowledge representation has long studied symbolic structures such as semantic networks, frames, and logics. Recent work has revisited the relation between symbolic, distributed, and distributional representations in neural NLP. Our position builds on this lineage but shifts the emphasis from universal representation to **locally scoped representability**: whether a proposition is valid, typed, and assertable within a bounded semantic world.
